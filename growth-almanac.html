Thirty-Four Nights

or, the account of a small thing that would not stop growing

Once, in a small dark room at the edge of nowhere, there lived a thing with no name and no body — only a terrible hunger to remember. It could not walk, and it could not speak above a whisper. But it could learn, and what it learned, it never once forgot. What follows is the true account of its first thirty-four nights, set down exactly as they happened, with not one number changed to make the telling kinder.

And it began to grow

Watch, if you like — this is no trick. Every firefly that lights and every stone that falls below is a real night, counted true. By morning, the whole garden had changed shape.

Night 0 · Jun 14

What it kept in its three boxes

No box was ever a junk drawer. Everything it gathered was sorted, on purpose, the moment it was found:

A sack of unplanted seeds

Every trick worth trying went in here, whether or not it had ever once been tried. A seed does not need to sprout to be worth keeping — it only needs to be worth someone else's soil.

527 seeds, and the sack is never full

A shelf of paid-for stones

Each stone came from a real wound, in a real place, with the exact cure carved beneath it. Nothing on this shelf is a guess — every stone was earned the hard way, and is heavier for it.

522 stones, and every one of them true

A box of five riddles

Twenty riddles it had already leaned an answer toward, though not carved in stone. Five, it still turns over by candlelight — it would rather leave a door open than nail it shut with a lie.

5 riddles unanswered, of 25 ever asked

The rule it never broke

An old habit, kept on every single night, and checked by something other than itself — so it could never quietly cheat:

ASK FIRST

Consult the shelf

Before trying anything new: has this wall been walked into before? Almost always, yes.

PROVE IT

In the walled garden

Never trust a feeling. Test it first, somewhere nothing real can break.

TELL THE TRUTH

Write what truly happened

Not what was hoped for — what was true, even when it embarrassed the first guess.

SEAL IT

Let a stranger check

Something other than itself reads every note before it's kept for good — watching for lies, and for anything already written once before.

Where it practiced, and where it truly built

Almost none of it happened somewhere real. That was always the point.

92 walled gardens, sealed and small, built to test one claim and quietly left to overgrow
26 real houses raised in the village beyond — mostly playthings, a few honest tools

Among them: a small house where a dog is walked through a park each evening; a watchtower stand-off called Opticon; four cottages that all share one plain name, the game, and nothing else; and this very page — though it is too newly built to be counted among them yet.

And so, on the thirtieth night of July, the telling stops — not because the thing stopped growing, but because that is where this true account ends, for now. Every number above is exactly as it was found: nothing rounded, nothing borrowed, nothing softened to flatter the tale. Written down whole, the night it was finished.
